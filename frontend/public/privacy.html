Iron Trainer — Privacy Policy

Last updated 21 August 2026

Iron Trainer is a personal triathlon training tool. It pulls your training and recovery data together, computes training load, and builds an adaptive plan. This page describes what it stores, where that data goes, and what it never does.

Two different situations, and they matter. Iron Trainer can be self-hosted — you run it on your own computer with Docker, and your data never leaves that machine except for the API calls you explicitly configure. It is also run as a hosted service at irontrainer.app. Where the answers differ, this policy says so.

Where your data goes

Iron Trainer sends data to exactly three kinds of destination, and nothing else:

  • Services you connect — Strava (to read your activities), and Apple Health via your own device. These only ever happen after you authorise them, and only for reading your own data.
  • Anthropic's Claude API, and only when an AI feature is enabled and you trigger it (generating or adapting a plan, the weekly check-in, race-day nutrition, or the WHOOP analysis). See the next section for exactly what is sent.
  • The database the application runs against — on your own machine if you self-host, or the hosted service's database if you use irontrainer.app.

Your data is not sold, rented, shared with advertisers, used to train any model, or used to build a profile of you for any purpose other than producing your own training plan. There is no analytics or tracking script on this site.

What is sent to the AI, specifically

This is worth being precise about, because "we use AI" usually is not. When an AI feature runs, the request contains:

  • Your training thresholds and heart-rate zones
  • Aggregate fitness numbers — chronic and acute load, form, weekly volume
  • The plan skeleton being adapted
  • For the WHOOP analysis: computed insights plus up to 90 days of daily figures (recovery %, HRV, resting HR, strain, hours slept, sleep performance)

It does not contain your name, your email address, your access tokens, or your individual activity records. Anthropic's handling of API data is governed by their privacy policy.

If no AI key is configured, these features fall back to a deterministic planner and nothing is sent anywhere. Self-hosted installations have no AI key by default — you have to add your own.

WHOOP data

Iron Trainer reads WHOOP recovery, strain, sleep and journal data either from an export file you upload or, if you connect it, through the WHOOP API using scopes you approve on WHOOP's own consent screen (read:recovery, read:cycles, read:sleep). It is used only to display your recovery alongside your training load and to compute your own behaviour correlations. It is never shared onward, and you can disconnect at any time from WHOOP's account settings or from within Iron Trainer.

How long it is kept, and how to delete it

  • Self-hosted: data lives in a database on your machine and is kept until you delete it. docker compose down -v destroys it permanently.
  • Hosted: data is kept while your account exists. Disconnecting Strava deletes your synced activities and everything derived from them, and revokes Iron Trainer's access to your Strava account. To delete everything, contact the address below.

Security

Tokens are stored server-side and never exposed to the browser. Session cookies are signed. The hosted service is served over HTTPS. A self-hosted install binds to your own machine only by default and is not reachable from your network unless you deliberately change that.

Iron Trainer is open source. If you want to check any of the above rather than take it on trust, the code is at github.com/gAmUssA/iron-trainer.
